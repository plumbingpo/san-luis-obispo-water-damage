The Science of Structural Drying in San Luis Obispo, CA

Home / Blog / The Science of Structural Drying in San Luis Obispo, CA

Ask people around San Luis Obispo, CA what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. This article explains the science behind professional structural drying and why it produces far better results than DIY attempts.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

The field behind professional drying is called psychrometry, which examines how mixtures of gas and vapor behave physically and thermodynamically. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. Every job is different, so technicians with IICRC certification calculate the setup psychrometrically, from air mover placement and target humidity and temperature to the number and kind of dehumidifiers.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

The equipment used for professional structural drying operates on an entirely different scale than consumer products. An hour of run time takes 15 to 30 or more pints out of the air with one LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifier, against 2 to 3 pints with a household model. High-velocity air movers create precisely directed airflow at 2,500 CFM or more to speed evaporation from wet surfaces. Under extreme moisture loads or in cold weather, the specialized tool is a desiccant dehumidifier. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

Each Stage of Professional Drying

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. The opening step is a full assessment: thermal imaging and meters locate and map each wet area. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned according to psychrometric calculations to create the best conditions for evaporation and moisture removal. Temperature may be raised to increase evaporation rates. Daily visits follow, with readings at each monitoring point, equipment adjustments where needed, and a record of how drying is going. The job is not finished until every wet material tests at an acceptable level against a dry reference standard.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

With the right commercial equipment, a typical home dries in 3 to 5 days. How long it takes comes down to how much moisture there is, which materials got wet (drywall dries far faster than concrete), how the building is constructed, the ambient conditions, and how many machines of what type are running. Typical figures are 5 to 7 days or more for concrete, 3 to 4 days for wood framing, and 2 to 3 days for standard drywall. After the first moisture readings, the technician gives you an estimated schedule and updates it as the job moves along.

Why Monitoring Matters

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. Surfaces can feel dry to the touch while holding dangerous moisture levels deep within the material. Only calibrated moisture meters can provide this verification. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
