Ceiling Water Damage Repair in San Luis Obispo

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • A smoke detector, register or fixture with water running from it
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Electricity Overhead

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

What We Do

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
