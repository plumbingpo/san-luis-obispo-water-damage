Water Damage We See Most in Riverside

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Riverside, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. Our Riverside technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away.
