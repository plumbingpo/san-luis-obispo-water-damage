Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in San Luis Obispo, San Luis Obispo Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in San Luis Obispo

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
